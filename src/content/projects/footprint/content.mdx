Footprint is a map that geolocates every branch of every FDIC-recognized bank in the FDIC datasets.

## Why I built it

A significant portion of my job involved competitive research, where I would look at other banks, specifically their Wikipedia pages. I found these really beautiful maps that were essentially made by one Wikipedia editor ([Magog the Ogre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User:Magog_the_Ogre)), who actually revealed how he made them. He made a tool that pulled the GPS locations and could regenerate these maps really quickly. And I thought, wow, I would like to be able to use this data to also fill in banks that he hasn't worked on.

So why didn't I do it back in 2021 when I discovered this? It's really hard to learn how to use different tools, especially visualization tools, especially when it's not a priority. So the quickest and easiest way was to make a Tableau, which we just used as an internal tool. A couple people would use it every year. Not that effective.

But with Claude, we could almost arbitrarily add complexity without increasing the cost of doing it. We could also make it not on Tableau, where Tableau would be slow; it takes a while to load data. We could do it natively in the browser as just another web page. And we could add features as we wanted.

## Comparing banks

One of the things we did with our Tableau, and one of the things Footprint enables you to do, is compare banks. It allows you to say: hey, this bank exists in this place, and this bank exists in this other place. What kind of merger is this? Is this a growth play, where banks are trying to make themselves a better deal for their customers by having a more expansive geography? Or is this an efficiency play, where these two banks are competing with one another, and maybe they're losing, and it would be easier if they just consolidated? Maybe they're all around the same blocks, getting hammered by the big banks, and it makes sense for them to merge. That's otherwise hard to tell, but with the map it's easier to see these differences.

## Build Your Own Bank

The Build Your Own Bank simulator is an idea I'd previously done purely mathematically, where I wasn't able to actually visualize what I wanted to see. It was really time-consuming. The idea is that you might want to see which bank is most geographically far apart from you. Each zip code is a dimension, and you want to say: on the dimension of all possible zip codes, which bank has the highest distance from me? Because if I'm growing purely organically, I want to add up all of the banks that are farthest from me until I match the entire unit of America.

The funny thing it spits out is that if you do this naively, you will add the Puerto Rican banks first, because they are by definition the farthest kind of bank that can exist.

## Scrollytelling

We also added a scrollytelling feature. It's easy to hear about mergers and acquisitions as the story that is guiding the banking industry, but it's hard to tell what those actually were, especially if you're entering the banking industry for the first time, or if you're young and naive. And even now it's hard to remember where different banks came from.

So with the scrollytelling feature we said: okay, let's go back and look at the dimensionality of banks over time. Something almost interactive, where you can say, oh, this bank existed here, it changed over time, and this is what they were doing. These are the moves that, for example, Jamie Dimon and Bank One were trying to perform.

## What's next

There's a set of possibilities. It'd be interesting to look at demographics: these banks are serving these demographics the best, or on the other hand, these are doing it worse. Claude gives everybody the access to answer questions around CRA really easily.
